About Reading

Reading is the process of converting written characters into meaning. It
is one of the oldest and most reliable methods of transmitting information
between people.

Why reading is used
- It is permanent. Unlike speech, it does not disappear after being produced.
- It can be read at any pace. The reader controls the speed.
- It can be revisited. Passages may be reread as often as necessary.
- It does not require the author to be present.

How reading works
1. Locate the text. In most cases this is done by opening a link.
2. Read from the beginning. Text is generally ordered so that it builds on itself.
3. If a word or section is unclear, continue reading. Context frequently
   resolves ambiguity.
4. If the meaning is still unclear after a complete read, read the document again.

Common questions

Q: What should I do with a link I received?
A: Open it. The linked document usually contains the information you need.

Q: Can I have the information summarized instead?
A: Generally no. The information is already written in the clearest form
   available, and a summary would omit details the author considered important.

Q: Is reading optional?
A: Reading the document in full is strongly recommended. It is unlikely to
   make sense in part.

Summary

If you were sent a link, read it. Most questions about a document are
answered by the document itself.
